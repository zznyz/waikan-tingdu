Photo 51: The Picture That Was Shown Without Asking
0:001:30

英 文 原 文 · 听 读
In 1952 an X-ray picture was taken in a London laboratory that would decide决定 the shape of DNA. It is known as Photo 51. The work was directed主持 by Rosalind Franklin; the exposure itself was made by Raymond Gosling, a research student in her group. The picture is a blurred cross of dark spots. To an untrained eye it means nothing. To anyone who could read it, the cross said helix, and the spacing of the spots gave the size. Franklin was careful. She wanted to prove the structure结构 from the evidence证据 rather than guess at it, and she was working her way there. Meanwhile her colleague同事 Maurice Wilkins showed the picture to James Watson without telling her. Watson later wrote that his mouth fell open. He and Francis Crick built their model模型 in Cambridge a few weeks afterwards. Here is the part usually left out. Franklin was not a woman who failed to understand her own photograph. Her notebooks show that she had already put the backbone of the molecule on the outside — the point Watson and Crick had got wrong in their first attempt. She published发表 her own paper in the same issue of Nature in 1953, and hers is the one that shows the data. She died of cancer in 1958, at thirty-seven. The Nobel Prize went to Watson, Crick and Wilkins in 1962, and that prize奖 is not given to the dead. So what was taken from her was never the prize. It was the chance to be asked.
参 考 译 文
1952 年，伦敦一间实验室里拍下了一张 X 光照片，而它将决定 DNA 的形状。这张照片被称为「照片 51」。这项工作由罗莎琳德·富兰克林主持；按下快门的，则是她组里的研究生雷蒙德·戈斯林。 照片上是一个模糊的十字形暗斑。在外行眼里，它什么也不是。而在读得懂它的人看来，那个十字说的是「螺旋」，斑点之间的间距则给出了尺寸。 富兰克林很谨慎。她想从证据里把结构证出来，而不是猜出来——她正一步步往那儿走。与此同时，她的同事莫里斯·威尔金斯把这张照片给詹姆斯·沃森看了，没有告诉她。沃森后来写道，他当场张大了嘴。几周之后，他和弗朗西斯·克里克在剑桥搭出了他们的模型。 这里有通常被略去的一段。富兰克林并不是一个读不懂自己照片的人。她的笔记本显示，她早已把分子的骨架放在了外侧——而那正是沃森和克里克第一次搭模型时弄错的地方。1953 年，她在同一期《自然》上发表了自己的论文；而给出实验数据的，正是她那一篇。 1958 年，她因癌症去世，年三十七。1962 年，诺贝尔奖授予了沃森、克里克和威尔金斯——而这个奖，是不授予已故者的。所以，被从她那里拿走的从来不是那个奖。是被征询的机会。
