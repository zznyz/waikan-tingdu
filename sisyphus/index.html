The Myth of Sisyphus
0:001:17

英 文 原 文 · 听 读
In ancient Greek stories, Sisyphus was a king who dared to trick the gods. As punishment惩罚, they forced him to roll a huge stone up a steep hill. Yet every time he neared the top, the stone rolled back down, and he had to begin again. His task was therefore endless无尽的: an eternal cycle of climbing and falling that could never be finished. For centuries this image stood for hopeless labour. To repeat重复 the same act forever, with no reward and no progress, seemed the cruellest fate命运 a person could suffer. Surely, people thought, such effort努力 was wasted in vain, and only a fool would refuse to give up. But modern thinkers have read the myth differently. They ask us to picture the moment when Sisyphus walks back down the hill. In that pause he is free; he understands his situation completely, and still he chooses to lift the stone once more. His struggle奋斗 is no longer a defeat but a decision. Seen this way, the value lies not in reaching the summit but in the determination决心 to keep going. Sisyphus turns his punishment into pride, and his heavy fate into a free act of will. If he can find purpose in a task that leads nowhere, then perhaps any life, however ordinary, can be meaningful有意义的—so long as we, too, refuse to stop pushing.
参 考 译 文
在古希腊传说里，西西弗斯曾是戏弄众神的国王。作为惩罚，众神罚他把巨石推上陡坡；每当快到山顶，石头就滚落回去，他只得从头再来，永无止境。几个世纪以来，它都象征着毫无希望的苦役：永远重复同一个动作、没有回报，似乎是最残酷的命运，只有傻瓜才拒绝放弃。但现代思想者读出了别样意味：想象他走下山坡的那一刻，他是自由的，看清处境后仍选择再次搬起石头——奋斗不再是失败，而是一个决定。价值不在抵达山顶，而在坚持下去的决心。他把惩罚化为骄傲，把命运化为自由的意志。若能在毫无结果的事里找到意义，那么再平凡的人生也可以有意义——只要我们拒绝停下推石的手。
