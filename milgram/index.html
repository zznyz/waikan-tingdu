The Number That Is Remembered Wrongly

英 文 原 文 · 听 读
In 1961 a young psychologist at Yale advertised for volunteers to take part in a study of memory. That was not what the study was about. Each volunteer志愿者 was told to read word pairs to a learner in the next room and to press a switch whenever the learner made a mistake错误. The switches were labelled in rising volts. The learner was an actor; the shocks were not real; the mistakes were planned in advance. The only thing being measured测量 was how far the volunteer would go. The number everyone remembers is that about two thirds of them went to the end of the board. It is the least useful number in the whole study. Stanley Milgram ran the experiment in more than twenty different arrangements, and the results moved enormously. When the learner sat in the same room, obedience fell. When the man in the grey coat gave his orders by telephone, it fell further. When two other volunteers refused拒绝 first, almost all of them stopped too. Obedience was not a fixed quantity in people; it changed with the arrangement of the room. Two other things are usually left out. The volunteers were not calm. They sweated, argued争论 and laughed in the wrong places; many begged to stop and were told to continue继续. And the records记录 show that the experimenter often pushed harder than the script allowed, which is one reason the findings are still argued over. The study is famous for a figure it did not really establish确立.
参 考 译 文
1961 年，耶鲁大学一位年轻的心理学家登广告招募志愿者，说是要做一项关于记忆的研究。这项研究其实根本不是关于记忆的。 每位志愿者被要求给隔壁房间的「学习者」念词对，学习者一答错，就按下一个开关。开关上标着依次升高的伏特数。可那个学习者是演员；电击不是真的；答错也是事先安排好的。唯一被测量的，是这位志愿者会走到哪一步。 人人都记得的那个数字是：大约三分之二的人一直按到了最后一档。而它恰恰是整项研究里最没用的一个数字。 斯坦利·米尔格拉姆用二十多种不同的设置做了这个实验，结果的差别大得惊人。学习者就坐在同一个房间里时，服从下降了。那个穿灰大褂的人改用电话下令时，又降得更多。另外两名「志愿者」先行拒绝时，几乎所有人也跟着停了下来。服从并不是人身上一个固定的量；它随着房间的布置而改变。 还有两件事通常被略去。被试并不平静。他们出汗、争辩，在不该笑的地方笑；许多人恳求停止，却被要求继续。而且档案显示，主试常常催得比脚本允许的更凶——这也是这项研究的结论至今仍有争议的原因之一。 这项研究出名，靠的是一个它并没有真正确立的数字。
