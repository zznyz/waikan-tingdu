How the Black Death Remade Europe
0:001:22
▶ 念到哪句，哪句高亮
英 文 原 文 · 听 读
In October 1347, trading ships from the Black Sea reached the ports of Sicily. Most of the sailors aboard were already dead or dying. Within four years the disease they carried — later called the Black Death — had killed, by most estimates, between a third and a half of Europe's population人口. The immediate consequences结果 were as terrible as you would expect. But the longer story is stranger: the catastrophe did not simply ruin Europe. It transformed彻底改变 it. Before the plague, land was scarce and workers were plentiful, so landlords could hold peasants to hard terms. Afterwards the arithmetic reversed: with so few hands left, there was a severe shortage短缺 of labour. The survivors were suddenly in demand需求. They bargained, they moved, and they won higher wages工资（按周/日结算的报酬）. Governments passed laws freezing pay at pre-plague levels; the laws largely failed, because a lord who refused to pay more simply lost his workers to one who would. Over the following century the old web of obligations began to decline衰落, and across much of Western Europe serfdom quietly collapsed瓦解. Historians still argue about how much a single epidemic can explain. But it is hard to look at the century after 1347 — freer labour, rising real wages, the first stirrings of the Renaissance — and not see a continent rebuilt by its own disaster.
参 考 译 文
1347 年 10 月，几艘来自黑海的商船驶入西西里的港口。船上的水手大多已经死去，或正在死去。四年之内，它们带来的那场疾病——后来被称为"黑死病"——据多数估计夺走了欧洲三分之一到一半的人口。眼前的后果，糟糕程度完全在意料之中。但更长的那个故事要奇怪得多：这场灾难并没有单纯地毁掉欧洲，它彻底改变了欧洲。瘟疫之前，土地稀缺而劳动力充裕，所以领主可以用苛刻的条件把农民捆在庄园上。瘟疫之后，这道算术题反了过来：活下来的人手太少，劳动力出现严重短缺，幸存的工人忽然变得有需求。他们讨价还价，他们迁徙，他们争到了更高的工资。各国政府颁布法令，要把工钱冻结在瘟疫前的水平；这些法令大多失败了——因为一个不肯加钱的领主，转身就会把工人输给愿意加钱的那个。此后的一个世纪里，旧有的人身依附之网开始衰落，在西欧的大部分地区，农奴制悄然瓦解。历史学家至今仍在争论一场瘟疫究竟能解释多少。但当你看向 1347 年之后的那个世纪——更自由的劳动力、上涨的实际工资、文艺复兴最初的动静——你很难不看见一片被自己的灾难重新砌起来的大陆。
